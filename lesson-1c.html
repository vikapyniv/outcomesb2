Unit 1C · Interactive speaking lesson

In the picture

Explore famous works of art, describe what you see and express interpretations using precise vocabulary and natural functional language.

Speaking · Lead-in

What is art?

1. Work in pairs. Read the quotations about art. Discuss what you think each quotation means. How far do you agree with each one? Explain why.
“Modern art = I could do that + Yeah, but you didn’t.”— Craig Damrauer
“Advertising is the greatest art form of the 20th century.”— Marshall McLuhan
“Art is eternal, but life is short.”— Evelyn de Morgan
“The more minimal the art, the more maximum the explanation.”— Hilton Kramer
Vocabulary

Talking about pictures

Which of these sentences about the painting do you agree with? Explain why.
1. The figure has his back to the viewer, which creates a feeling of mystery.
2. He looks as if he is completely lost in thought.
3. He is obviously a confident and adventurous person.
4. He seems to be the most important element in the painting.
5. He appears to be looking for something beyond the landscape.
6. I get the impression that the scene is more emotional than realistic.
7. The landscape looks like a symbol of uncertainty or freedom.
8. It must be somewhere high in the mountains; it could well be early morning.
Speaking · Practice

Famous paintings

Open a card. Answer the three questions and try to use the Functional Language and Vocabulary shown for that painting. Click a vocabulary pill to flip it and see a very short meaning.
1. Mona Lisa — Leonardo da Vinci
Italy / France • portrait • c. 1503–1519
Functional language
I get the impression...She seems to...
Vocabulary · click to reveal
  1. What makes her expression difficult to interpret?
  2. What details create the strongest first impression?
  3. Why do you think this portrait became so famous?
2. The Starry Night — Vincent van Gogh
Netherlands / France • landscape • 1889
Functional language
It looks as if...It creates...
Vocabulary · click to reveal
  1. What mood does the sky create?
  2. Which part of the painting attracts your attention first?
  3. Does the scene feel calm or intense to you? Why?
3. The Scream — Edvard Munch
Norway • expressionist scene • 1893
Functional language

Vocabulary · click to reveal

  3. What could the underlying message be?

Netherlands • portrait • c. 1665

Vocabulary · click to reveal
