Guillermo del Toro’s Pinocchio

You’re probably familiar with the tale of Pinocchio: the wooden puppet that dreams of being a real boy and whose nose grows every time he lies. The classic story has proved popular source material for numerous films and remakes since Disney produced their version of the Carlo Collodi classic in 1940, but there is none other quite like this one. Get ready for a Pinocchio created with Guillermo del Toro’s magic.

The story doesn’t seem a surprising choice for Mexican director Guillermo del Toro. A dark fairytale with child characters set in a cruel world is just the kind of subject matter he seems to like to deal with and echoes themes from his other successful films, such as Pan’s Labyrinth.

Despite the often dark moral teachings of many classic fairytales, modern adaptations tend to be more uplifting. However, with this version, del Toro has chosen to be more faithful to the Italian setting of Carlo Collodi’s original tale while mixing it with elements of fantasy. He deals with mature themes in the same honest, realistic way we have come to associate with del Toro. Although some aspects may be attractive to children, there is some death and stronger themes in the film which sometimes contrasts with the lighter musical numbers. Since it is rated PG, none of the scenes are very violent, but some could be considered disturbing for younger viewers. Although Pinocchio is often considered a children’s story, del Toro himself claims the film wasn’t made with kids in mind. Although children will undoubtedly enjoy the amazing 3D characters, del Toro’s Pinocchio is more of a treat for adults.

The result of Guillermo del Toro’s collaboration with fellow director Mark Gustafson is a beautifully made film that deals with strong themes of sadness and loss in a meaningful, uplifting way. This is Pinocchio like you’ve never seen him before - very realistic and yet extremely fantastical at the same time. Once again, Guillermo del Toro has proved himself a genius in the world of film.

Hi guys, I’m Florian Schulz and this is my channel, Art ‘n Travel where I share recommendations about where to go to see amazing art.

With the days about to get shorter and darker, I thought I would tell you about some of the best light shows to see to brighten up your life this winter.

First up is the Kadriorg Festival in Estonia’s capital. Get ready to light up your September evenings with a walk through the different parks of the city of Tallinn. You’ll see more than 1000 lights, high-tech lasers and fireworks in various displays to illuminate the natural beauty of this city and to welcome the dark winter months in a positive way. And guess what? The entrance is completely free. So, if you’re in Tallinn in September, you have no excuse not to go.

Next, we have the Magic Water Circuit in Lima, Peru. This display explores the amazing things that can be created when you combine coloured light and moving water. For a small fee, you can see 13 fountains illuminated and choreographed to music. The water looks as if it’s dancing to the music. The whole experience is very uplifting, though you should probably be prepared for a few wet surprises.

This list wouldn’t be complete without telling you about the Mizu Akari festival in Kumamoto, Japan. For one weekend in October, the castle district of the city is decorated with over 3500 bamboo shoots and 54,000 candles while hundreds of little lights are placed in the water all around the castle. The effect is very atmospheric. You certainly won’t want to miss this magical experience if you can.
