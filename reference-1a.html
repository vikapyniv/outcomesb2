Habits

A clear guide to talking about things we regularly do now, things we did in the past, frequency, and the mistakes to avoid.

Present habitsPast habitsFrequencyCommon mistakes
Present

Present habits

We generally use the present simple. We can also use tend to + verb.

I listen to music all the time.
I tend to watch films on demand these days.
Two negative forms are possible:
I don’t tend to go to the cinema that much.
I tend not to go out during the week.

Emphasising a contrast

Use do / does + verb in an affirmative sentence when you want to emphasise a contrast to a habit.

I’m mainly vegetarian, but I do occasionally eat meat.
Past

Past habits

Use the past simple, used to + verb, or would + verb. Used to and would (often contracted to ’d) have a similar meaning for repeated past actions.

I listened to music all the time when I was younger.
Did you use to watch cartoons when you were a kid?
I used to play computer games all the time.
I didn’t use to go out much.
Remember: there is no final -d after did / didn’t: Did you use to…? and didn’t use to… With never, keep it: never used to. Questions and negatives with would are less common.

Past states

Use used to, not would, for a past state.

I used to have long hair.
I would have long hair.
How often?

Frequency and comparisons

Adverbs and adverbial phrases help us show how frequently a habit happens.

We usually eat out a couple of times a month.
I mostly eat vegetarian food as a rule.
I guess I eat meat maybe once every couple of weeks.
I hardly ever read books these days.
Useful comparison: don’t … as much as + clause
I don’t go out as much as I used to.
We don’t see each other as much as we’d like to.

You can also finish the phrase with I want to or we did before.

Watch out

Common mistakes

1 · Don’t use the past continuous for a past habit.
I was going swimming a lot when I was younger.→I went / used to go swimming a lot when I was younger.
2 · Don’t use would for a past state.
I would have long hair when I was younger.→I used to have long hair when I was younger.
3 · Use as, not how, in this comparison.
I don’t go how much I’d like to.→I don’t go as much as I’d like to.
